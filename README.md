Hi, I am currently working on making a remote control roll of filament, (basically a robot disguised as your classic 1KG roll of 3d printer filament). The concept is fairly simple, have two separately driven wheels with a center hub with a very low weight to stay upright (if you need a reference go watch Jurassic park and look at the gyro balls). My original design used the yellow TT motors, but those would have been extremely slow as I wanted it to go at least walking speed (theoretically) meaning I had to switch to higher power motors. The problem with the larger motors is that they would not fit horizontally in the frame, so I added some bevel gears to change the output, and now everything should fit perfectly. I don't have a reason for doing this project other than just for fun or to gain experience and try to prank someone that their filament is running away. A while after starting this project I wanted to see if this had ever been done before and after looking for a while I found that one other person has, Matt  Denton. I watched through his video on how he made it (https://youtu.be/6ZLJA1YpoMs?si=HU7LwZJHynkEuFAy) and I was pretty impressed, his build was extremely high quality and very well built. However he built his into a 2kg roll, whereas my design is built around a 1kg roll meaning I have about half the space. Some key differences in mine vs his is that I am using a much smaller battery, saving space and that my motors are geared vertically to save space, whereas he is able to have them horizontally. Another difference is the motor choice, he uses encoded motors in order to get highly reactive robot with advanced stuff such as autocorrection and keeping its angle. I believe that this would be overkill from mine and that I would be alright with non encoded motors and just an IMU for angle measurement. This is my first ever hack club project and I hope I get everything submitted right. Thanks to whoever ends up reading this, Dominic.
